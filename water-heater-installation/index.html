Before any pipe is cut, we size the heater to your household. For tanks, first-hour rating counts more than gallons; for tankless, it is gallons per minute at your winter temperature rise. Next, we choose the spot together. Gas models require a vent route outdoors and adequate combustion air. A full-size relief valve discharge line runs to a safe point, and any required expansion tank, drain pan, seismic straps, or permit is part of the job. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A heater squeezed into a closet or attic where a leak would do real damage
  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater
  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • Morning showers that run cold for the last person
  • An older install without an expansion tank, pan, or straps where required
  • A property where the water heater has to go in from scratch
  • Interest in tankless or heat pump efficiency

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Installing a new water heater means doing several trades' work at once. Gas piping has to be sized for the heater's input and every other appliance on the line, then pressure-tested and leak-free. The vent must meet the manufacturer's rules on diameter, length, pitch, and clearances, or carbon monoxide can find its way indoors. Electric and heat pump units need a circuit, breaker, and wire gauge matched to their load, and whole-house electric tankless units can draw more current than many panels have to spare. On the water side, there is more: shutoffs, dielectric transitions, a correctly rated relief valve with a full-size discharge line, and code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps that differ from place to place.

A professional installation covers the whole job, not just setting the heater. Sizing by first-hour rating or flow rate, tested gas piping, a vent routed to spec, a circuit matched to the load, and the expansion tank, pan, or straps your code requires are all included. Gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check before we leave. You end up with an installation that is safe, properly sized, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, we will tell you directly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
