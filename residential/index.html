Upfront Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

No homeowner likes surprises on the final bill. So Corvallis Water Heater prices every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the venting and supply lines. You'll know the problem, what the fix involves, and what it costs before you say yes.

A lot of water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Keep an eye out for hot water with a rusty tint, a rumbling tank, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a spent anode rod, or a failing dip tube. An early service call at the first sign often costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The date code on the label usually gives it away. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth scheduling a replacement before the current one decides for you. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Annual Flushing

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service heads off failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Corvallis Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your heater's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

Consider maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already bought. Each visit helps keep the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod working, and the relief valve ready to open, which lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most families, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

An undersized water heater runs out halfway through the second shower, while an oversized one wastes energy keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Corvallis Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your morning rush, rather than simply copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute against the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

Choosing a new water heater is about more than the number of gallons. Our technician will compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and a properly sized tank can be the simpler, less expensive choice. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Most service calls we get come down to a handful of recurring problems. There's no hot water at all. The pilot lights and then goes out a minute later. The shower goes cold after a few minutes, the tank rumbles and pops, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. Our technicians handle all of them, always targeting the component that actually failed.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element only cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve spitting water, on the other hand, signals that pressure or temperature inside the tank is too high, and water pooling under the tank can soak into flooring before anyone notices. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and call the gas utility first. We come prepared to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, add a thermal expansion tank when pressure is the culprit, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
