How Salem Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Salem

Around Salem, water heater trouble tends to trace back to a handful of causes. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be a real factor: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A standard tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been used up, leaving the steel open to rust. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, which means we service both sides: thermocouples, burners, and venting on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Homes on private wells may bring iron, sulfur, and grit into the tank, and a rotten-egg odor in the hot water often means a reaction with the anode rod that a different rod can fix. Older homes often need code upgrades when a heater is replaced: a thermal expansion tank on closed systems, a correctly piped T&P discharge line, a drain pan where a leak would hit finished floors, and seismic straps where local code requires them. All of these is fixable, and most are much cheaper when caught early.
