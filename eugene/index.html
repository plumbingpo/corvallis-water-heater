Keeping Your Eugene Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Common Water Heater Problems in Eugene

A water heater in Eugene almost never fails out of nowhere. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound during heating, brownish hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
